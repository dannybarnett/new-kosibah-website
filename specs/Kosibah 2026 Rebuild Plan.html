2. Objective and principles

Objective. Turn a visitor into a booked consultation with Yemi, in person in Brooklyn or over Zoom. Everything on the site earns its place by moving someone toward that, or by making them trust the outcome enough to do it.

  • Show the process, not just the dress. Sketching in real time, the toile, the fittings, hand beading. This is what separates couture from a boutique, and no competitor's site shows it well.
  • Outcome as proof. Real brides in real venues, with their own words. This section carries the conversion.
  • Phone first. Portrait images in 4:5 and 9:16, one column, thumb-reach buttons, a persistent "Book" link in the header.
  • Small and fast. Six pages, one purpose each, under one megabyte per page, no third-party scripts except the Meta pixel and the calendar on the booking page.
  • Nothing that can expire. No plugins, no licences, no theme updates. Content is plain files that Claude Code or a simple editor can change.

4. The booking funnel

One page, kosibah.com/book, is the destination for every channel. Ad and social traffic never lands on the home page.

Step 1: the form

First name, email, phone or WhatsApp, wedding date, in person or Zoom, an optional note, consent. Submitting sends a "Lead" event to Meta, emails the atelier, and adds a row to a leads sheet. It should take under a minute on a phone.

Step 2: the calendar

An embedded scheduler (Cal.com recommended; Calendly or Acuity work the same way) showing Yemi's real availability, with name and email pre-filled from step 1. Booking creates the calendar event, sends confirmations, adds a Zoom link where relevant, and sends a "Schedule" event to Meta through its Conversions API, which is more reliable than the browser pixel inside the Instagram app.

5. Design direction

Fashion editorial, not bridal boutique. Chalk-white ground, near-black type, one oxblood accent used only on the booking action. A high-contrast Didone serif for headings, a light geometric sans for everything else. Photography does the talking; type stays small, spaced and quiet.

  • Layouts. Nine artboards on the canvas: site structure, six phone pages, the second booking step with confirmation, and a desktop home. Phone artboards are full-page scrolls at 390 px.
  • Photography placeholders. Each image slot is labelled with the shot it needs, so the canvas doubles as the shot list for the photographer.
  • Motion. One: a slow crossfade or a short muted video loop in the home hero. Nothing else moves.
  • Accessibility. Real buttons and labels, 44 px touch targets, text at 4.5:1 contrast, reduced-motion respected.

The canvas is private until shared from its Share menu. Yemi will need access to comment.

6. Imagery and content

Reuse now

The Asheyori Eji lookbook is portrait 2:3 and crops cleanly to 4:5 for phones. The ten Real Brides sets cover the outcome. The Contact hero (the Manhattan Bridge shot) is strong enough for the desktop home. These are enough to launch.

Commission: the atelier shoot

One day at the atelier with a bride or model, shot for phones. This is the long pole in the timeline, so book it first.

  • Yemi sketching with a bride, the sketchbook, the finished illustration.
  • Fabric: bolts of silk, lace, beaded appliqué in the atelier's light.
  • The toile on the stand with chalk marks and pins.
  • A fitting in the mirror, hands with pins.
  • Macro of a needle through beaded lace; corsetry inside a bodice.
  • Portrait of Yemi at the cutting table.
  • Six to eight short vertical video clips, five to eight seconds each, for the hero and for Meta ads.

Deliver in 4:5 and 9:16 crops plus a 3:2 for desktop, consistently graded. Model and photographer releases for web and paid social.

Copy to gather from Yemi

  • Couture lead time and the number of fittings, for The Atelier and gown pages.
  • Whether the first consultation is free or carries a fee. The mockups assume no charge; confirm before build.
  • One to three sentences from each Real Bride, and each wedding's venue and photographer.
  • Names and short notes for the couture gowns in the Turner, Bienvenue, Tuscany, Stateside and Chrysallis collections.

7. Technical approach

Recommended: static site, git-based editing

LayerChoiceWhy
FrameworkAstroShips plain HTML with no JavaScript by default. Built-in image optimisation (AVIF/WebP, responsive sizes). Claude Code builds and maintains it directly.
ContentMarkdown files plus KeystaticGowns, brides and press are folders of files with images. Keystatic gives Yemi a simple editing screen over the same files, no database.
HostingCloudflare PagesFree tier, global CDN, automatic deploys from GitHub, redirects file for the old URLs.
BookingCal.comEmbeds well, syncs Google Calendar and Zoom, webhooks for the Meta event. Free for one person. Calendly is the equivalent if preferred.
Form handlingA Cloudflare Pages FunctionEmails the atelier (via Resend), appends to a Google Sheet, fires the Meta Conversions API. About fifty lines of code, no service to pay for.
AnalyticsCloudflare Web Analytics plus Meta PixelNo cookie banner needed for Cloudflare's; the pixel loads only on /book and with consent text on the form.
CodeGitHub repositoryEvery change is versioned. Claude Code can add a gown, a bride or a press item from a sentence.

Running cost. Domain plus, at most, a Cal.com or email plan if a paid tier is chosen. Everything else is on free tiers. Compared with WordPress hosting, theme and plugin licences, this is close to zero.

Trade-off. Day-to-day edits go through Danny (a sentence to Claude Code) or through Keystatic's editor. There is no drag-and-drop page builder, which is the point: nothing can drift off-brand or break.

Alternative: Squarespace with Acuity Scheduling

Hosted, Yemi can edit everything, and Acuity is built in for bookings. It costs a monthly fee, the templates fight an editorial layout, and the page weight is higher. Choose this only if it matters that Yemi can restyle pages without Danny.

Not recommended: another WordPress build

The current failures are all plugin and theme rot. A leaner theme would help for a year and then need the same care again.

8. Performance, SEO and measurement

  • Targets. Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 s on a mid-range phone on 4G; total page weight under 1 MB; Lighthouse performance above 90 on every page.
  • Redirects. Every old URL mapped to its new home: collections to gown pages, the-founder and in-the-press to /yemi, contact and bookings to /book, blog posts to the home page.
  • Structured data. LocalBusiness with the Brooklyn address and hours; Person for Yemi; each gown as a Product without a price.
  • What we measure. Visits to /book, form completions (Lead), calendar bookings (Schedule), and consultations held. Report monthly by channel using the UTM tags.

Ongoing

Maintenance

  • Roughly an hour a month: add a bride or a gown, check the booking flow, read the channel report.
  • No updates, licences or renewals apart from the domain.

10. Decisions needed

  1. Stack. Astro on Cloudflare (recommended) or Squarespace with Acuity.
  2. Scheduler. Cal.com (recommended), Calendly, or Acuity.
  3. Consultation policy. Free or a fee, length, and whether Zoom consultations are still offered.
  4. Loungewear and Mother of the Bride. Drop loungewear or link to a shop; keep Mother of the Bride as a gallery filter.
  5. Photography. Budget and a date for the atelier shoot, and who the bride or model is.
  6. Who edits. Danny via Claude Code only, or Yemi as well via Keystatic.
  7. Access. WordPress admin for the export, the domain registrar, the Meta Business account and Instagram, and Yemi's Google calendar for the scheduler.
